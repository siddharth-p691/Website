Is tutoring right for your child right now?

Every kid has a rough week. The real question is whether what you're seeing is a normal dip, or a gap that will keep showing up until something changes.

Tutoring is very good at closing a specific skill gap, building test confidence, teaching study systems, and catching a concept before it snowballs into the next unit. It's not a fix for a mismatched class level, an undiagnosed learning difference, or a scheduling and workload problem — those are worth ruling out first, often with a quick conversation with the teacher.

A simple test: if your child understands the material with you sitting next to them, but not alone, that's usually a confidence and practice gap — exactly what tutoring is built for.

Signs worth paying attention to

  • Grades in one subject have dropped over more than one grading period, not just one bad test.
  • Homework that used to take 30 minutes now regularly takes 90 or more.
  • Your child avoids a subject, or says "I'm just bad at it" more than they used to.
  • They can do the homework with help, but freeze on tests without it.
  • A concept from earlier in the year still hasn't clicked, and new material keeps building on it.
  • Report card comments mention "capable of more" or "not completing work."

If three or more of these are true, it's worth a conversation. Book a free call →
