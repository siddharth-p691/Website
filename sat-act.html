Free roadmap

A roadmap for starting test prep in 9th grade.

Spread across four years, not crammed into one.

The goal isn't four straight years of test prep. It's spreading the same total effort across four years instead of cramming it into six stressful months of junior year. Here's how that actually breaks down, grade by grade.

The four-year outline

9th grade

Foundation, not tests yet

Build strong study habits and close any gaps in core math and reading skills. A baseline practice test is useful here just to see a starting point, not to prepare intensely.

10th grade

The PSAT as a dry run

Take the PSAT for practice and early exposure to the format. Start light, consistent test-skill practice alongside regular coursework — no cramming yet.

11th grade

The year that counts most

This is typically when the PSAT counts toward National Merit consideration, and when most students take the SAT or ACT for the first time, often more than once. AP exams for junior-year courses land here too. This is the year the earlier groundwork pays off.

12th grade

Polish and apply

Any final retakes, application-specific requirements, and using senior year for applications rather than starting test prep from zero.

The goal isn't to do more work overall — it's to make sure no single year has to carry all of it.
